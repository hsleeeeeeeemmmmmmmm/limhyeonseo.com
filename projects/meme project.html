Between endless replication and consumption, an interrogation into contemporary exhaustion and the absence of aliveness.

Having grown up immersed in digital culture, the memes that erupt and dissolve into silence have always struck me as strange, quasi-biological entities. Rather than sustaining a continuous life, their existence is driven solely by the frantic impulse to replicate—surviving only to be consumed by an anonymous audience. Tracing their fleeting life cycles leaves me with a quiet, lingering grief, rooted in a sudden moment of recognition: I see myself mirrored in them.

We exist in a passive loop where we numbly consume intuition-driven, thoughtless stimuli while simultaneously rendering ourselves objects to be consumed by others. In a reality where validity is achieved only through endless circulation and consumption, a chilling question arises: Are we truly alive? By mirroring the hollow life cycle of the meme, this project confronts the absence of aliveness—rendering a self-portrait of contemporary exhaustion, pervasive numbness, and modern burnout.
