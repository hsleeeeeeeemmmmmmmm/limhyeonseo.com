A quick visual exercise exploring the tension between seeing and being seen through shifting gazes.

Developed as a short, one-day visual study, Doppelgänger is a spontaneous moving-image experiment centered on the gaze. The project playfully examines how a simple shift in camera angle and eye line can alter the perception of identity and presence.

By placing the act of looking at the core of the video, the work creates an uncanny dynamic between the viewer, the subject, and the lens. Rather than presenting a heavy narrative, this brief exercise tests how the camera can quickly duplicate, fracture, and mirror the self—turning a routine classroom prompt into an intuitive play with visual perspectives.
